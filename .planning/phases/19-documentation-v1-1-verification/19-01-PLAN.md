### OD-2 — The pending-confirmation dead end (`19-RESEARCH.md § Critical Finding`, Open Decisions (b))

**Resolved: Option 2 — configure the VER-01 demo tenant so the Actor's low-value skip
short-circuit provably engages on the refund leg; record the residual gap explicitly
rather than silently.** Implemented and proven in `19-02-PLAN.md` Task 2; recorded as
accepted threat T-19-04 and written into `19-UAT.md` by `19-05-PLAN.md`.

Rationale — Option 1 (build a `pending_confirmations` approve/reject route) is not the
"plain FastAPI route against the existing ORM model" that `19-RESEARCH.md` assumed. Read
against source, approval has a structural problem the research did not surface:
`_execute_transactional_tool` (`apps/api/app/services/transactional/tools.py`) reaches
`call_actor_gate` at Step 5 on **every** invocation. A resolve route that honours the
threat-model requirement to "re-enter the dispatcher's checks rather than call the
adapter directly" would therefore re-run the Actor, which would return `require_human`
a second time — approval would loop rather than complete. Making approval terminate
requires a new human-approved bypass seam threaded through the dispatcher at exactly
the position the threat model warns about, which is materially more than a route. That
is a **context-cost and dependency** constraint (a security-critical seam designed with
no live control DB available in this environment to verify it against), not a judgement
that the work is difficult — see `<planner_authority_limits>`. It is also uncovered by
any of this phase's five requirement IDs.

The residual gap is therefore **accepted, not closed**, and is made a tested fact rather
than a claim: `19-02-PLAN.md` Task 2 asserts that the demo tenant's `place_order`
envelope does **not** engage the skip, so `require_human` remains reachable on that leg.
Follow-up is logged to v1.2 in STATE.md by `19-05-PLAN.md` Task 3.
